What Is Monday.com? (And Who Should Use It?)

CCBy Charlie Company Editorial Team·Aug 4, 2026·5 min read

Somewhere between your third group text thread about a project and your fifth spreadsheet that nobody's updating anymore, someone probably told you to try Monday.com.

And honestly? That's not bad advice.

Monday.com is genuinely useful. For some businesses, it's a game changer. For others, it's a beautifully designed tool that solves problems they don't actually have.

So before you sign up, let's talk about what it actually does, who it's built for, and whether it makes sense for a business like yours.

What Monday.com Actually Is

Monday.com is a work management platform. Fancy way of saying: it's a place where teams can organize tasks, track projects, and see who's doing what.

Think of it like a giant interactive to-do list that your whole team can see and update in real time. You can build boards for different projects. Assign tasks to people. Set deadlines. See what's done and what's still in progress. Color code everything so it looks great in a screenshot.

It's visual. It's flexible. And it's genuinely well designed.

Forbes Advisor's full review of Monday.com gives it high marks for its clean interface and the sheer number of ways you can customize it. If your business runs on projects with moving parts and multiple people involved, Monday.com is worth knowing about.

What Monday.com Does Really Well

It Makes Team Collaboration Way Easier

If you've ever had a project fall apart because nobody knew what anyone else was doing, Monday.com fixes that.

Everyone sees the same board. Tasks have owners. Deadlines are visible. Progress updates happen in one place instead of scattered across emails and texts. For a team working on a shared project, that clarity saves a lot of time and a lot of frustration.

It's Incredibly Flexible

Monday.com works for almost any kind of project because you can build it however you want. Marketing campaigns. Product launches. Construction timelines. Event planning. Software development. You name it.

Project-management.com's independent review of Monday.com highlights this flexibility as one of its biggest strengths. The platform doesn't force you into a specific workflow. You design the boards that match how your team actually works.

It Connects to Other Tools You're Already Using

Monday.com plays well with others. It integrates with Slack, Google Drive, Zoom, Gmail, and dozens of other tools. If your team lives in those apps already, Monday.com can plug right in.

Where Monday.com Falls Short

It's Built for Projects, Not Customers

This is the big one.

Monday.com is great at tracking work. It is not a CRM. It doesn't track customer relationships. It doesn't remember when you last contacted someone. It doesn't send automated follow-up messages. It doesn't manage your reviews or help you show up on Google.

If your business lives and dies by customer relationships, Monday.com is only solving part of your problem.

It Gets Complicated Fast

Real users on Reddit who reviewed Monday.com after using it long-term point out that the platform can feel overwhelming once you're past the basics. The more boards you build, the harder it gets to keep everything organized. And if you don't have someone dedicated to maintaining the setup, things get messy quickly.

For a small business where the owner is also the project manager, the sales team, and the person answering the phone, that overhead adds up.

Pricing Climbs as You Grow

Monday.com's free plan is limited to two people. Once you need more seats or more advanced features, the price jumps. For a small team, you might find yourself paying for a tool that still doesn't handle half of what your business actually needs.

Who Monday.com Is Perfect For

To be fair, Monday.com is an excellent product for the right user.

It shines for creative agencies, marketing teams, tech companies, and any organization running complex multi-step projects with a team of five or more. If your daily reality involves campaign timelines, product development sprints, or coordinating deliverables across departments, Monday.com is probably worth every penny.

It's also great for operations-heavy businesses where internal task management is the main challenge.

Who Might Need Something Different

If you run a home service business, a trade company, a local retail shop, or a small professional practice, your needs are different.

You don't need a project board. You need customers to find you. You need leads to get followed up with automatically. You need reviews to keep coming in. You need past customers to remember your name when they need you again. You need your schedule, your billing, and your marketing to talk to each other.

Monday.com doesn't do any of that.

It's a tool built for managing internal work. Not for managing the customer side of a local business.

The Question Worth Asking

Before you pick any software, ask yourself this: what problem am I actually trying to solve?

If the answer is "my team can't stay organized on projects," Monday.com might be your answer.

If the answer is "I'm not getting enough leads," "I lose track of customers," "my follow-up is inconsistent," "I don't show up on Google," or "I don't know which jobs are actually making me money," then you need something built for that. And Monday.com isn't it.
